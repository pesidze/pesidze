§ 01How it works

I have a full-time job, so non-profit projects run alongside it. Each project gets 20–30 hours per month — enough for social media design, a website, print materials, and similar work. I work pro bono, with a few conditions that keep the collaboration comfortable for both of us.

  • Official contract required.Even for free work, I ask for a signed agreement. It protects both parties and lets me document volunteer work officially. I issue a proper invoice marked at €0.
  • All tasks in writing.Briefs, feedback, change requests — everything via email or a shared document. This helps me plan my time and not lose details.
  • 3–5 business days per task.I combine this with my main job. Please plan realistic timelines from the start.
  • Up to 2 rounds of revisions.If additional rounds are needed, we discuss them separately.

§ 02What I don't take on

Even with the best intentions, some situations don't fit within my constraints:

  • Urgent tasks.“Needed yesterday” does not fit around a full-time job.
  • Projects without a clear brief.I need to understand what I'm designing and why before I start.
  • Daily availability.Work that implies on-call responsiveness.

§ 03What you get

Within 20–30 hours per month I can cover:

  • Social media.Posts, stories, templates.
  • Web.Website design or updates.
  • Print.Posters, leaflets, event programmes.
  • Decks & documents.Presentations and similar graphic work.

§ 04How to apply

If this sounds like a fit — write to me with a short description of your organisation and what you need. I’ll tell you whether I have capacity right now and if the project is something I can take on.

Every engagement — even pro bono — comes with an official contract. That’s how I keep things professional and how you can use our collaboration as documented in-kind support.
