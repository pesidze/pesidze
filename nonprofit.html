How it works

I have a full-time job, so non-profit projects run alongside it. Each project gets 20–30 hours per month — more than enough for social media design, a website, print materials, and similar work. I work on a pro bono basis, with a few conditions that make the collaboration comfortable for both of us.

  • Official contract required. Even for free work, I ask for a signed agreement. It protects both parties and allows me to document volunteer work officially. I issue a proper invoice marked at €0.
  • All tasks in writing. Briefs, feedback, change requests — everything via email or a shared document. This helps me plan my time and not lose details.
  • 3–5 business days per task. I combine this with my main job. Please plan realistic timelines from the start.
  • Up to 2 rounds of revisions per deliverable. If additional rounds are needed, we discuss that separately.

What I don't take on

Even with the best intentions, some situations simply don't work within my constraints:

  • Urgent tasks — "needed yesterday" does not fit around a full-time job.
  • Projects without a clear brief — I need to understand what I'm designing and why before I start.
  • Work that implies daily availability or on-call responsiveness.

What you get

Within those 20–30 hours per month I can cover: social media design (posts, stories, templates), website design or updates, print materials (posters, leaflets, event programmes), presentation decks, and similar graphic work.

Every engagement — even pro bono — comes with an official contract. This is how I keep things professional and how you can use our collaboration as documented in-kind support.

If this sounds like a fit — write to me with a short description of your organisation and what you need. I'll tell you whether I have capacity right now and if the project is something I can take on.
